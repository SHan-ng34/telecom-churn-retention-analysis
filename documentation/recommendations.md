# Business Recommendations

## Executive Conclusion

The analysis indicates that churn and revenue exposure are concentrated within specific customer segments rather than being evenly distributed across the customer base.

Month-to-month customers and early-tenure customers represent substantial areas of observed churn and revenue exposure. The combination of month-to-month contract, electronic check payment, and 0–12 month tenure shows particularly high observed churn.

Rather than applying a broad retention program, the business should test targeted interventions within high-risk active segments. These interventions should be evaluated using incremental retention, revenue retained, intervention cost, and net incremental value.

This approach moves the analysis from identifying where churn is concentrated to determining where retention investment can be tested and economically justified.

---

## Recommendation 1: Early-Tenure Retention

### Finding

Customers in their first 12 months show substantially higher observed churn.

- Customers: 2,186
- Churned customers: 1,037
- Churn rate: 47.44%
- Historical monthly revenue associated with churn: $68,954.25

Within the month-to-month and 0–12 month segment:

- Customers: 1,994
- Churn rate: 51.35%
- Churned customers: 1,024

### Recommended Action

Test a targeted early-tenure retention program consisting of:

- Structured onboarding
- Proactive early customer check-ins
- Early service/support outreach
- Targeted offers where economically justified
- Relevant product/service education

### Target

Prioritize active customers within their first 12 months, particularly those on month-to-month contracts.

### Measurement

Evaluate:

- Retention rate
- Incremental retention versus control
- Revenue retained
- Intervention cost per customer
- Net incremental value

## Recommendation 2: Contract and Payment Conversion

### Finding

Month-to-month customers using electronic check show a high concentration of observed churn and revenue exposure.

- Customers: 1,850
- Churned customers: 994
- Churn rate: 53.73%
- Historical monthly revenue associated with churn: $77,315.60

Electronic check also remained positively associated with churn in the multivariable logistic regression model (OR ≈ 1.53, p < 0.001).

### Recommended Action

Test interventions designed to:

- Reduce payment friction
- Encourage automatic payment adoption where appropriate
- Communicate the value of longer-term plans
- Test targeted incentives for movement from month-to-month to longer-term contracts

### Target

Prioritize active month-to-month customers using electronic check, with additional focus on early-tenure customers.

### Measurement

Evaluate:

- Incremental retention
- Contract conversion rate
- Payment-method conversion rate
- Revenue retained
- Intervention cost
- Net incremental value

## Priority Pilot Segment

The intersection of the two opportunity areas provides a focused population for initial testing:

### Month-to-month + Electronic Check + 0–12 Months

- Total customers: 954
- Churned historically: 602
- Observed churn rate: 63.10%
- Historical monthly revenue associated with churn: $43,703.15
- Active customers: 352
- Active monthly revenue exposure: $22,368.40

This segment should be treated as a potential pilot population rather than as proof that a particular intervention will cause retention.

## Retention Economics

Illustrative six-month scenarios were used to evaluate the economics of intervention.

| Intervention Cost / Customer | Assumed Retention | Break-even Retention |
|---:|---:|---:|
| $20 | 10% | 5.24% |
| $40 | 15% | 10.49% |
| $60 | 20% | 15.73% |

These scenarios are not forecasts. They provide decision thresholds for evaluating future retention experiments.

An intervention should be scaled only when observed incremental value supports the associated cost.

---

## Experiment Framework

A future retention test should follow:

1. Define eligible customers.
2. Randomly assign eligible customers to control and treatment groups.
3. Apply the selected intervention to the treatment group.
4. Measure retention over the defined evaluation period.
5. Calculate incremental retention versus control.
6. Estimate revenue retained.
7. Subtract intervention costs.
8. Evaluate net incremental value.
9. Scale, modify, or discontinue the intervention based on observed results.

---

## Analytical Limitation

The dataset is an observational customer snapshot. The analysis identifies associations between customer characteristics and observed churn but does not establish causation.

Therefore, the recommendations should be treated as hypotheses for targeted experimentation rather than as proven causal solutions.

The historical revenue-at-risk figures represent monthly revenue associated with customers who churned in the observed dataset. Active-customer revenue exposure is used when discussing forward-looking retention opportunities.
