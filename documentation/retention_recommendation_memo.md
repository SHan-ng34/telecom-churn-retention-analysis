# Customer Churn & Retention Economics

## Retention Recommendation Memo

**To:** Telecom Subscription Business — Retention / Customer Strategy Team  
**Subject:** Prioritizing retention opportunities using churn and revenue exposure  
**Analysis scope:** 7,043-customer historical snapshot

---

## Executive Summary

Customer churn is **26.54%**, while customers who churned were associated with **$139.1K in historical monthly revenue exposure**. Revenue exposure is therefore higher than the customer churn rate alone would suggest.

The analysis shows that churn and revenue exposure are concentrated across specific customer segments, particularly **month-to-month customers, newer customers, and electronic-check users**. The intersection of these characteristics identifies a potential pilot population for targeted retention activity.

---

## Key Findings

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---:|
| Overall churn | **26.54%** |
| Historical monthly revenue exposure | **$139.1K** |
| Month-to-month churn | **42.71%** |
| 0–12 month churn | **47.44%** |
| Electronic-check churn | **45.29%** |
| Priority segment churn | **63.10%** |
| Active customers in priority segment | **352** |
| Active monthly revenue exposure | **$22.4K** |

**Priority segment:** Month-to-month + Electronic Check + 0–12 months.

This segment combines elevated observed churn with identifiable active customers who remain potentially retainable.

---

## Recommended Actions

### 1. Strengthen Early-Tenure Retention

Focus onboarding and proactive support during the **first 12 months**, particularly for month-to-month customers.

Potential actions:

- Structured onboarding and check-ins
- Proactive service or support outreach
- Product/service education
- Targeted retention offers where economically justified

### 2. Test Contract and Payment Conversion

For relevant month-to-month customers, test interventions that encourage longer-term plans or reduce payment friction, including appropriate promotion of automatic payment options.

The analysis identifies **associations**, not causal effects, so these should be treated as hypotheses to test rather than proven drivers of churn.

---

## Economics & Next Step

Using an illustrative **6-month retention horizon**, a **$40 intervention cost** and **15% assumed retention rate** produces:

- Potential revenue retained: **$20,131.56**
- Intervention cost: **$14,080**
- Net potential value: **$6,051.56**
- Break-even retention rate: **10.49%**

These figures are **scenario economics, not observed experiment results**.

### Proposed Next Step

Run a controlled retention pilot among eligible customers:

**Eligible population → random treatment/control assignment → measure incremental retention → calculate revenue retained and intervention cost → evaluate net incremental value.**

Scale only if the observed incremental economics support the intervention.

---

## Analytical Limitation

The dataset is an **observational historical customer snapshot**. It can identify associations and revenue exposure but cannot establish that any characteristic causes churn.

An actual A/B experiment could not be conducted from this dataset because it contains no treatment assignment, control/treatment groups, intervention timing, or post-intervention outcomes.
